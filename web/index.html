Your projector and its current settings

Enter what the projector menu shows now. The app records each change you make and works out the best value for every setting. Projectors keep separate settings per picture mode, so SDR and HDR each get their own profile.

Number ranges are starting guesses. If your menu runs −50…+50 or 0…20, change Min/Max/Default to match. That keeps the suggestions in your projector's own units.

Connect the projector and the camera

A. How patterns get to the projector

  1. Mac: Control Center › Screen Mirroring › your Apple TV › choose Use As Separate Display.
  2. System Settings › Displays › the Apple TV display: pick the resolution that matches the Apple TV output (3840×2160 / "looks like 1920×1080" for 4K).
  3. Drag it onto the projector display and click inside it to go full screen.

Pattern window not open.

  1. Mac: Control Center › Screen Mirroring › your Apple TV › Mirror Built-in Display. In Displays settings, choose "Optimize for: Apple TV" so the picture is 16:9 and fills the screen.
  2. Patterns cover this whole browser window.
  3. While a pattern is up: Space measures, Esc or a click returns to the app. Instructions also appear at the top of the pattern.

The app watches the camera while you play the generated pattern videos on the Apple TV itself. That bypasses the Mac and AirPlay entirely and is the only way to test HDR.

  1. Generate the videos once: python3 tools/make_videos.py (in the ProjectorCal folder; about 2 minutes).
  2. Get them onto the Apple TV: AirDrop them to an iPhone and AirPlay from the Photos app, or import them into Photos (iCloud Photos then shows them in the Apple TV Photos app), or play them from a media app such as Infuse that reads a shared folder.
  3. Each video starts with black/white flashes, then holds its pattern for 3 minutes. Use "Locate from video flashes" below as it starts.

B. Camera

Getting a good camera view
  • Best: an iPhone as a Continuity Camera, on a tripod or propped at your seat, landscape, screen facing away from the projector screen. It is far more sensitive in the dark than the MacBook camera, and you keep the laptop in front of you.
  • iPhone not in the list? Lock it and set it down, landscape and still, near the Mac. If it still doesn't appear, Chrome may not see Continuity Camera at all: open the app in Safari, which does. Each browser keeps its own settings, so move them with Export JSON / Import JSON on the Report page. In Safari, set Safari › Settings › Websites › Camera › localhost to Allow so it stops asking.
  • With the MacBook camera, point the lid at the screen. In mirror mode the instructions show on the projector.
  • Turn off Center Stage, Portrait, Studio Light and Reactions: Control Center › Video Effects while the camera is on. They crop and alter the picture.
  • Frame the whole projected image so it fills most of the view, with a little margin. Don't move the camera after locating the screen.
  • Room fully dark and the camera roughly at your seating position. Keep the camera itself out of the light beam.

C. Find the picture in the camera view

Check the camera preview: the green outline should sit exactly on the projected image. Drag its corners to fine-tune.

SDR calibration

Work top to bottom. For each step: Measure, make the change the app asks for on the projector, close the projector menu, and measure again until it says done. Space measures the highlighted step, Y/N answer by eye.

HDR: why it looks barely better than SDR

Most "HDR looks flat" problems are signal-chain settings rather than calibration, so check these first. Answer what you can; the likely causes update as you go.

HDR measurements (generated videos on the Apple TV)

Switch the projector to its HDR mode (play the video; with HDR set to Auto it switches by itself). The HDR profile in Setup holds the HDR-mode settings. Locate the screen from the video's opening flashes if the camera has moved.
